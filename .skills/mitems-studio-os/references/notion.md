# Reference Teardown: Notion

> **Essence:** Notion looks like a well-set document, not an app. Chrome recedes almost to nothing; content is the interface. A restrained black-on-white palette is warmed by signature hand-drawn/illustrative accents, giving a powerful, flexible tool a friendly, unintimidating face.
> **Source:** https://notion.so · analyzed 2026-07-08 · confidence: inferred (well-established public design language)

> ⚖️ **Copying-line status:** PASS — principles only. Notion's illustrations, wordmark, and copy are NOT reproduced.

## 1. Design Philosophy
**The document is the UI.** Notion minimizes chrome so users focus on their content; the tool's enormous flexibility is tamed by a calm, editorial surface and gentle onboarding. Warmth (illustration, rounded friendliness) offsets the potential intimidation of an "everything app."

## 2. Typography Breakdown
- **Family:** a clean, highly readable sans (system/UI sans) tuned for long-form reading; strong document typography (clear headings, comfortable measure, good body leading).
- **Hierarchy:** editorial — think "well-typeset page," not "dense dashboard." Generous line-height; clear H1/H2/body.

| Role | Size (approx) | Weight | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Page title | ~40px | 700 | document-like |
| Heading | ~20–28px | 600 | editorial |
| Body | ~16px | 400 | ~1.5 leading, comfortable measure |

## 3. Color Palette
- Predominantly **black-on-white** with soft grays; minimal accent used functionally; illustration provides warmth/color. A subtle, paper-like calm.

## 4. Spacing Scale
- Generous, reading-oriented; whitespace makes a dense feature set feel airy. Content blocks separated clearly.

## 5. Grid System
- Capped **reading-width** content (constrained measure); marketing pages use simple centered columns with illustration accents. Content-first, not chrome-first.

## 6. Motion Language
- Subtle, functional (block drag, hover reveals, gentle transitions). Nothing flashy — keeps focus on content.

## 7. Interaction Patterns
- **Minimal persistent chrome**; controls appear on hover/near content (progressive disclosure). Slash-command creation; drag-and-drop blocks; everything feels lightweight and inline.

## 8. UX Principles
- **Content over chrome.** **Progressive disclosure** hides power until needed. **Warmth to reduce intimidation.** **Editorial typography** for a reading tool.

## 10. Things to Avoid
- Hover-revealed controls can be **undiscoverable** and are problematic on touch/for keyboard/AT users — ensure discoverability + accessible alternatives ([`22`](../22-ACCESSIBILITY.md)).
- "Infinite flexibility" can overwhelm onboarding — strong templates/defaults matter.
- Minimal chrome must still expose critical actions clearly.

## 11. How to Recreate This Style Without Copying
1. **Minimize chrome** and let content dominate; reveal advanced controls progressively — but keep them **discoverable and accessible** (not hover-only).
2. Use **editorial typography** with constrained measure for reading-heavy surfaces.
3. Add **your own illustration style** for warmth (not Notion's specific illustrations).
4. Provide **templates/defaults** to tame flexibility.
5. Keep motion subtle and functional.
6. Diverge with your own accent, illustration, and voice.
