# Reference Teardown: Coursera

> **Essence:** Where Duolingo gamifies, Coursera **legitimizes**. It signals academic credibility with a calm, trustworthy **blue** palette, prominent **university/company partner logos**, ratings/enrollment numbers, and clear credential outcomes (certificates, degrees). The design is content-dense (a large course marketplace) but organized, readable, and accessibility-conscious.
> **Source:** https://coursera.org · analyzed 2026-07-08 · confidence: inferred (well-established public design language)

> ⚖️ **Copying-line status:** PASS — principles only. Coursera's blue as-such, wordmark, partner logos, and copy are NOT reproduced.

## 1. Design Philosophy
**Trust and legitimacy** are the product for online education — will this credential be respected? Coursera's design answers with restraint, institutional cues (partner logos, instructor credentials), social proof (ratings, learner counts), and clarity about outcomes. Blue is chosen for its trust/competence associations; the tone is professional, not playful.

## 2. Typography Breakdown
- **Family (inferred):** clean, highly legible sans (Coursera uses a custom "Source Sans"-derived family); optimized for long-form reading and dense catalogs.
- **Hierarchy:** clear course titles, partner/instructor names, ratings, and outcome labels; readable body for descriptions. Understated — content leads.

## 3. Color Palette
- **Trust blue** as primary/brand/action; white canvas; grays for structure; gold/amber rating stars; restrained semantic colors. Calm, professional, credible — the opposite of Duolingo's saturation.

## 4. Spacing Scale
- Comfortable, content-friendly; card grids with consistent gaps; enough density to browse a large catalog without clutter.

## 5. Grid System
- Capped content; **course-card grids** (thumbnail + partner + title + rating + outcome), filter/facet sidebars, carousels by topic. Responsive, catalog-oriented.

## 6. Motion Language
- Restrained, functional: hover states, carousel transitions, progress indicators in-course. Motion supports navigation and progress, not spectacle.

## 7. Interaction Patterns
- **Course card** as the core unit (thumbnail, partner logo, title, rating, enrollment, credential type). Strong search/filter/facets; clear enrollment CTAs; progress tracking in-course; credential/certificate emphasis.

## 10. Things to Avoid
- Trust-blue is **ubiquitous in edtech/SaaS** — risks looking generic; differentiate via type, imagery, or a secondary accent ([`02`](../02-PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md), copying line).
- Content-dense catalogs can overwhelm — strong IA/faceting + clear hierarchy essential ([`27`](../27-INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md)).
- Credential/outcome claims must be **honest** (accreditation, job outcomes) — inflated claims are an ethics/legal risk (Art. III).
- Ratings/enrollment numbers must be **real** ([`17`](../17-LANDING_PAGE_DESIGN.md)).

## 11. How to Recreate This Style Without Copying
1. For credibility-sensitive products, build **trust scaffolding**: real institutional/partner logos, credentials, honest ratings/outcomes.
2. Choose a **palette by emotional goal** (trust/competence → blue family, *your* shade) — and add a distinguishing secondary so you're not generic.
3. Use a **card + faceted-search** catalog pattern with strong IA for density.
4. **Lead with outcomes** (what the learner gains).
5. Keep it **accessible** and readable for long content.
6. Diverge with your own type, imagery, and accent; decide playful (Duolingo) vs. serious (Coursera) per positioning.
